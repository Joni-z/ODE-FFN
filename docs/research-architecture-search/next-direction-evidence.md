# What remains unresolved after the operator and complete-field studies

The strongest remaining explanation is how the network learns and shares nonlinear features across inputs and time, rather than a missing pointwise gate direction, conditional-label noise alone, or a large violation of the target's PSD structure. This is a working explanation, not an approximation lower bound. The next architecture experiment should distinguish a specific feature-interaction mechanism; another order, rate-bound or controller-width sweep would revisit questions already tested.

## Where the current gap sits

A new read-only calculation uses the same 30 Stage17 arrays: three arms, Ring8/Product16, five times and all 512 anchors per panel. It reproduces native field errors and retains every input-norm quartile in [one CSV](next-direction-time-panels.csv), with [input hashes and definitions](next-direction-time-panels.provenance.json). No target labels or new model outputs were generated.

On Product16, t=.5 and .7 contribute **83.69%** of the parent's five-panel field-error excess over SwiGLU128; t=.9 contributes 12.39%. At t=.7, parent MSE is .728068 versus .260577. Relative to the oracle velocity's mean squared magnitude, these are 25.51% versus 9.13%; the discrepancy is not an artifact of dividing by a nearly zero target signal. The corresponding denoiser residual is `m*-x=(1-t)v*`, so its error/signal ratio is identical at a fixed time.

The gap is distributed across input norms: its four quartile shares are 24.50%, 24.55%, 23.85% and 27.09%, aggregated across the five times. At t=.7 even the lowest-norm quartile has parent/SwiGLU MSE .75348/.26591. On Ring8 the parent instead beats SwiGLU128 at t=.1 and .3, with its main deficit at .7. A shared final-LayerNorm bound therefore does not explain the observed relative ranking by itself. These finite norm quartiles describe the saved distribution, not asymptotic tails; neither panel metric is the separate uniform-time primary oracle MSE.

## What earlier controls actually exclude

**Noise-only and simple coordinate explanations are insufficient.** [Stage10](population-noise-readout.md) uses matched fresh-population inputs for pair/mean supervision. Removing conditional label noise leaves Product16 ODE error .183114 versus .012699 for the better SwiGLU. This rejects noise as a sufficient account under that budget, without separating optimization from representation. Conversely, [Stage11](clock-coordinates-readout.md) improves the same function class and initial function by changing clock coordinates, so optimization geometry matters. Its failure to catch SwiGLU blocks treating that coordinate change as the solution.

**Neither sharing nor SwiGLU multiplication alone explains everything.** [Shared P/controller features](analysis-shared-features/analysis-report.md) worsen performance; widening an independent controller still loses at matched compute. Yet [ordinary expanded SiLU](analysis-reference-silu/analysis-report.md) reduces Product16 error from .266155 to .153892 at the parent's exact budget, while worsening Ring8. Thus a generic feature-transform benefit exists on Product16, but neither expansion nor gating is isolated as the universal cause. [Stage20](analysis-forced-gate/analysis-report.md) preserves SwiGLU and learns an active small correction without useful improvement; it supplies no evidence for changing the activation curve through higher terms.

**Legal Jacobian shape still leaves the fitting problem.** [Complete-field projection](analysis-complete-field/README.md) assigns only 1.24% of the Product16 parent's Jacobian error to skew/negative-spectrum distance; its pointwise PSD-projected error remains 49.302 versus native SwiGLU128's 23.958. Most mismatch remains inside the allowed symmetric geometry. That does not bound a retrained constrained model. Likewise, [controller tangents](analysis-controller-tangent/analysis-report.md) already show full pointwise vector-gate span; tiny-bank tangent fits fail to transfer for both families. Repeating a pointwise rank argument would not identify the shared functional restriction.

## Ranked next actions

1. **Review one original-controller feedback question before implementing anything.** The coupled SwiGLU-generator proposal has been rejected; it is not the next experiment. A distinct question now under review compares the original frozen clock `D(h)P` with the state-dependent drift `f(z)=D(z)Pz`, using frozen K2 versus explicit midpoint that recomputes the same controller at the midpoint. Derive the missing state-clock derivative and identify a matched control before selecting training. Count repeated evaluations and compare against both established SwiGLUs. Improvement must address the measured middle-time discrepancy while retaining low-time performance. Full pointwise vector-gate span is already available, so “more independent output directions” is not sufficient motivation.
2. **Keep the JiT head comparison as a compatibility question, not the leading repair.** The [head review](head-control-design-review.md) exposes real objective, initialization and endpoint differences. The norm/time evidence weakens a tail-based rationale for immediate X/S/V training. The [saved-head envelope check](analysis-head-envelope/README.md) also places the parent's output-range floor at zero on Ring8 and only 0.0992% of actual Product16 field error. A selected head control must use one common, well-defined target and report within-head ODE/SwiGLU interactions; `m=x+(1-t)N` alone is exactly the old velocity model. Do not bundle head, sampler, clipping and FFN changes.
3. **Use learning trajectories to distinguish slow fitting from a persistent interaction deficit in that one selected experiment.** Preserve fixed checkpoints and the full time panels, rather than add widths or a second rescue family. Existing finite-budget failures do not prove global capacity limits; a new architecture must earn a quality/compute advantage, not merely reduce a diagnostic violation.
