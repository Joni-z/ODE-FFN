# Focused reading list and implications

Checked on 2026-10-21. This is a targeted primary-source review, not an exhaustive survey or a claim that these are the newest papers in every subfield. Selection is based on the specific mathematical gaps in ODE-FFN. The two 2026 theory/optimization preprints below are labeled as such; recency does not establish correctness or importance. External papers do not establish our method's efficacy.

## Read these first

**1. Back to Basics: Let Denoising Generative Models Denoise — Tianhong Li and Kaiming He.** [Paper, version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.13720v2), November 2025 / January 2026 revision. Read the prediction-target comparison, bottleneck analysis and Algorithm 1. JiT argues for clean-data prediction and tests a low-dimensional patch bottleneck. This identifies the architectural object we actually want to improve: a conditional denoiser. It does not imply each FFN should be a linear ODE. The current repository's denoiser follows this x-prediction construction, whereas our toy experiments use direct velocity prediction. Full text and these sections were inspected.

**2. Lipschitz regularity in Flow Matching and Diffusion Models: sharp sampling rates and functional inequalities — Arthur Stéphanovitch.** [2026 preprint](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.06065v1). Read the assumptions, one-sided Lipschitz setup and especially Appendix B.1, Proposition 9. It expresses velocity derivatives through posterior covariance and distinguishes one-sided growth control from full operator norm. This is directly relevant to deciding what “stable enough” means. Its results assume target/path regularity; they do not prove that clipping a neural hidden P improves generated samples. Relevant theorem statements, assumptions and covariance derivation were inspected; this review is not an independent verification of all proofs.

**3. Preconditioned Flow Matching — Shadab Ahamed et al.** [2026 preprint, version 2](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.02337v2), revised May 13. Read Sections 3.1–3.3 and Remark 3.6. The Gaussian regression analysis connects the intermediate covariance spectrum to optimization rates. It motivates separating optimization geometry from representational restrictions when discussing our polynomial P parameterization. The precise results concern linear predictors/GD and related stylized settings, not arbitrary AdamW-trained networks. The mixture optimization discussion assumes perfect component assignment and uses a centered covariance mixture; it cannot be transplanted unmodified to our separated-mean Ring8. Relevant statements and proofs for Gaussian regression were inspected.

**4. On the Guidance of Flow Matching — Ruiqi Feng et al., ICML 2025.** [Published record](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/feng25s.html), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.02150v3). Read Section 3.4 and Appendix A.12 rather than implementing guidance. Their use of posterior covariance and denoiser derivatives is a useful established connection between denoising and vector-field geometry. It helps ground the identity used in our mathematical note. The paper's contribution is guidance, not an ODEFFN architecture; its results cannot be cited as evidence for our gate. Relevant sections and publication metadata were inspected.

## Use these to test the novelty and direction of our story

**5. Mamba: Linear-Time Sequence Modeling with Selective State Spaces — Albert Gu and Tri Dao, COLM 2024.** [Full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.00752v2). Read Sections 3.2 and 3.5.1, Theorem 1 and the selective-parameter ablation. It already makes an explicit connection between input-dependent discretization and gating. Thus “learn a duration depending on input” is not, on its own, a novel motivation. Our potential distinction would have to concern a specific polynomial operator restriction and its utility for the FM/denoising target. Mamba has sequential state, forcing and a different architecture; it is not equivalent to this tokenwise FFN. The structural sections were inspected. This older paper is included for prior-art relevance, not presented as frontier news.

**6. Gated Attention for Large Language Models: Non-linearity, Sparsity, and Attention-Sink-Free — Zihan Qiu et al., NeurIPS 2025.** [Published paper record](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/904e89bb4e632e75fb47f093b620b257-Abstract-Conference.html). This provides a strong alternative interpretation: input-dependent gating can help for generic representation/optimization reasons outside flow matching. We need to distinguish such an explanation from FM-specific adaptation. It studies attention in language models, not our FFN or our objective. Only the official abstract and publication record were reviewed here; no mechanism claim from its full experiments is assumed.

**7. Mean Flows for One-step Generative Modeling — Zhengyang Geng et al., NeurIPS 2025.** [Published record](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2025/hash/6d13e085b79d454da5910e4ca82a3d9d-Abstract-Conference.html), [full text](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.13447v1). Read Sections 3–4.1: it defines a target quantity, derives an identity involving that target and trains against it. This is a useful example of the mathematical connection our project currently lacks. It is not a reason to replace our task with one-step generation. The average-velocity identity concerns external time intervals, not repeated powers of a hidden P. Relevant definitions and derivation were inspected.

**8. Flow Matching for Multimodal Distributions — Gaoxiang Luo et al., CVPR 2026.** [Authors' project and theory summary](https://mm-flow.github.io/), [author's publication record](https://gaoxiangluo.github.io/). The work changes the source distribution and data coupling to exploit multimodal geometry. This matters because Ring8 difficulty depends on the chosen independent Gaussian-to-mixture path, not just on a model's generic ability to represent multiple modes. The theorem summary has explicit separation/support assumptions; our full-support Gaussian components do not satisfy a disjoint-support hypothesis. The publisher PDF returned 403, so only author-provided methods/theorem summaries and metadata were verified. Do not claim a full-paper review.

## Foundational anchor, not a new recommendation

[Flow Matching for Generative Modeling](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.02747), Lipman et al., ICLR 2023, establishes the conditional/marginal regression framework. Its basic objective is needed to distinguish architecture claims from numerical-flow claims; there is no need to package it as a recent discovery.

## What these papers collectively change in our reasoning

The useful connection is **target geometry → required derivative/representation behavior → a restricted architecture or optimization argument**. A superficial match between the word “flow” in the objective and in an FFN is insufficient. Input-dependent dynamics/gating already has substantial prior art. Meanwhile, posterior covariance, denoising targets and path conditioning provide concrete mathematical quantities that our existing analytic datasets can expose.

No paper reviewed here establishes that sharing P across Taylor terms is the right restriction for FM. That remains the actual research gap to evaluate, not an assumed premise. This is not an exhaustive novelty search or a claim that no related method exists.
